### 1. FlowOps
**Enterprise Request Management System**

**Role:** Product Designer  
**Timeline:** 2 weeks  
**Tools:** Figma, Adobe Creative Suite  
**Deliverable:** Hi-Fi Prototype

#### Overview
End-to-end product design focused on workflows, roles, and decision clarity in enterprise tools.

#### Problem
Internal requests get lost. Approvals stall. Nobody knows who owns what.

- **The Pain:** Requests submitted via email, Slack, and spreadsheets. No single source of truth. Approvals buried in inboxes.
- **Who Suffers:** Employees waiting weeks for equipment. Managers drowning in approval requests. IT teams triaging without context.
- **Why It's Hard:** Multiple roles with conflicting needs. Compliance requirements. SLA contracts with real penalties. Scale across departments.

#### Design Challenge
How might we design a system where request ownership is always clear, approvals never stall invisibly, and every user sees exactly what they need to do their job, without drowning in stuff they don't?

#### Design Goals
- Eliminate ambiguity around request ownership and status
- Support 4 distinct user roles without duplicating workflows
- Make high-volume request triage fast and reliable
- Surface SLA deadlines and bottlenecks before they become crises

#### Key Screens
- Request Dashboard
- Request Detail View
- Agent Triage Queue
- Workflow Management

### 3. HABITat
**Gamified Habit Tracking App**

**Award:** Most User-Centered Design

**Role:** UX Designer  
**Duration:** 4-Week Sprint  
**Tools:** Figma, FigJam  
**Team:** 4 Designers (Deepa Bhat, Anusha Ramachandran, Vaishnavi Chandrapati, Aaron Foronda)

#### Overview
Track your habit, build your habitat. A gamified approach to habit tracking for college students.

Most habit tracking apps fail because they lack incentives, rewards, and accountability. HABITat reimagines habit tracking through gamification. As you maintain your habits, you build a virtual habitat and unlock adorable animal companions. The app combines reward systems with social accountability, making habit-building feel less like a chore and more like nurturing your own little world.

#### Problem Statement
How might we design an app for college students that tracks their habits in a clear and concise way while keeping them engaged and accountable?

#### Design Process
- Week 1: Research & Discovery (user surveys, competitive analysis, stakeholder interviews)
- Week 2: Ideation & Concept (crazy 8s, dot voting, affinity mapping)
- Week 3: Wireframing & Testing (low-fi sketches, mid-fi wireframes, usability testing)
- Week 4: Hi-Fi & Presentation (visual design, interactive prototype)

#### Research Insights
- **Incentivization Gap:** Users need tangible rewards and visual progress to stay motivated beyond the first week
- **Social Accountability:** Social connections and shared goals create external motivation
- **Emotional Rewards:** Gamification elements tap into intrinsic motivation when done meaningfully

#### Key Features
- Collectible animal rewards through habit milestones
- Shared habits with friends
- Safari-themed visual design
- Streak tracking with recovery options

#### Results
- 30% increase in user engagement during testing
- 94% of testers would use HABITat regularly
- Won "Most User-Centered Design" Award

#### Key Learnings
- Gamification requires connecting rewards to real progress
- Social features dramatically improve retention
- User-centered design wins over assumptions

### 5. Spotify Redesign
**Concept Redesign for Meaningful Music Discovery**

**Role:** UX Designer  
**Duration:** 2 weeks  
**Tools:** Figma, Cursor, Lovable  
**Platform:** Mobile (iOS)

#### Overview
Spotify helps you find more music. But what if it helped you understand why you listen?

I noticed a pattern in my own listening: saved songs became forgotten archives, recommendations felt random, and music turned into background noise. This concept redesign explores what Spotify could look like if it prioritized meaningful listening over infinite discovery. I introduced a concept called Listening Threads: finite, behavior-driven collections that explain why they exist and help you rediscover music that matters.

#### Problem Statement
How might we transform music streaming from passive consumption into active reflection, helping listeners understand their emotional relationship with music while still enabling effortless discovery?

#### Design Process
- Research
- Ideation & Sketches
- Lo-Fi Wireframes
- Mid-Fi Prototypes
- Hi-Fi Design & Iteration
- Usability Testing
- Final Polish

#### Research Findings
- 72% of listening is background/passive
- 78% of users forget songs they've saved
- 65% find recommendations feel opaque
- 35% want more context about their music

#### Key Features
- **Listening Threads:** Finite, behavior-driven collections with clear explanations
- **Why This Exists:** Every recommendation comes with a human-readable explanation
- **Listening Memory:** Visual patterns of emotional listening over time
- **Emotional Clusters:** Songs organized by feeling, not just genre
- **Rediscovery Moments:** Resurface forgotten favorites with personal context

#### Results (User Testing)
- 87% of users preferred Threads over current discovery
- 4.6/5 average rating for Listening Memory feature
- 92% said they'd use Emotional Clusters regularly

#### Key Insight
"This makes Spotify feel personal again." - User testing feedback
